Practice

My practice is rooted in biotechnology and marine molecular biology, with a particular focus on fungi, seaweeds and microbial systems. I combine experimental research with collaborative and interdisciplinary approaches, working from molecular and cellular processes to biological materials, marine biomass and the development of research formats that connect different fields of knowledge.

Across these contexts, I am interested in how microorganisms interact with complex biological materials, how biological processes can support new uses of marine resources, and how scientific knowledge and experimental practices can contribute to interdisciplinary research and collaboration.

Research areas

Marine fungi & fungal biotechnology

My doctoral research focused on marine fungal diversity, physiology and extracellular enzyme systems. I work with cultivation, physiological characterisation and comparative molecular approaches to investigate fungal responses to seaweed and other complex biomasses, with particular interest in their potential for marine biotechnology and biological processing.

Seaweeds & marine biomass

A major strand of my work concerns Saccharina latissima and other marine biomasses: their biochemical variability, interactions with microbial processes, and opportunities and limitations in food, protein-transition and biorefinery contexts. This work connects fundamental biological characterisation with questions of processing, resource use and application.

Microbiology & microbial systems

I work with fungi and bacteria across marine and other experimental systems, from cultivation and growth characterisation to environmental microbiology, microbial physiology and biomass–microbe interactions. Earlier work included marine bacteria and mercury bioremediation; current collaborations also extend into microbiome research.

Molecular biology & genetics

My molecular biology experience includes DNA-based identification, molecular markers, sequence analysis and comparative workflows, together with earlier CRISPR-based research. I use these approaches as part of broader experimental questions rather than as an isolated molecular platform.

Microscopy & biological imaging

Microscopy is both a scientific method and a recurring bridge into interdisciplinary collaboration in my work. My experience includes confocal and fluorescence microscopy, scanning electron microscopy and expansion microscopy across marine microbiology, virology and artist–scientist research.

Fermentation, bioprocessing & biochemical analysis

I work with microbial cultivation and controlled bioreactor experiments, physiological and enzymatic screening, and the evaluation of biological conversion of biomass. Analytical experience includes SDS-PAGE, reducing-sugar assays, HPSEC, HPAEC and ATR-FTIR, alongside biochemical profiling of biomass and microbial culture supernatants.

Interdisciplinary & transdisciplinary practice

Alongside experimental research, I develop interdisciplinary work with artists, designers, cultural organisations and scientific partners. I am interested in collaborations where different disciplines shape the questions, methods and outcomes of the research. Scientific materials and methods remain active components of the work: organisms, images, data and laboratory processes can become shared research tools.

Project development & coordination

I work on the development and coordination of collaborative research projects, particularly where scientific, technical, cultural and societal partners need to work across different vocabularies and methods.
